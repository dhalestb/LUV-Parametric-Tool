How to read the evidence

Blue is source-supported floor within the original review boundary. Orange marks overhead geometry below seven feet. Violet marks open sky. A white ring locates one sampled minimum. Cyan traces a passing local crossing and amber marks an unresolved proposal. A gold destination and converging circulation become available after verification in the Evaluation tab.

Each floor footprint is the union of coplanar OBJ triangles clipped to the selected one-foot cells. The outlines can end at sampling boundaries inside a larger floor. Headroom areas use projected, height-clipped triangles. Reported minimum and median heights use covered sample centers. A circular fit inside the seven-foot-clear floor measures local width independently of the rectangular plan span. Thin vertical faces divide patches. Seven feet is the comparison band. The two-foot route strip checks continuous support, headroom and step rise with geometric and sampled tests. Four transverse offsets up to 0.4 ft allow for crossings off the sample centerline. Repeated crossings of the same boundary do not establish independent circulation branches. The OBJ files have one architecture object and no room, stair or occupancy labels.
